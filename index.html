Tax Signal Screen

Type a US-listed company. The screen ranks its latest annual report against companies of similar size and shows the reported numbers that moved its rank most.

This is a screening aid, not an allegation. A high rank means the company's reported numbers resemble those of companies the SEC later charged with misstating accounts. Most highly ranked companies have done nothing wrong. Use it to decide where to look first.

How well it works

Each year from 2015 to 2020, the model was trained only on earlier years and then asked to rank that year's filings. The figures compare its ranking with the misstatements the SEC later announced. It is better than chance and comparable to published research models, but it misses many cases and flags many clean companies.

Method
  • Data: SEC Financial Statement Data Sets (XBRL), annual 10-K filings, fiscal 2009–2024; companies with at least $10 million in total assets.
  • Labels: SEC Accounting and Auditing Enforcement Releases, matched to the fiscal years each case says were misstated.
  • Score: RUSBoost ensemble (300 trees, three random seeds) on 27 reported line items plus 19 ratios, including tax signals such as the book–tax gap, effective tax rate, cash taxes paid and the tax–earnings growth gap.
  • Reasons: a separate tree model trained to reproduce the scores from the readable ratios alone (it reproduces 87% of the variation on held-out companies), explained with SHAP values. Reasons describe what drove the score, not proof of anything.
  • Size: raw scores rise with company size because enforcement cases skew toward large companies, so the rank compares each company only with the tenth of companies closest to it in total assets.
Limits
  • Enforcement cases take years to surface, so recent years have few labels and the model learns mostly from 2009–2018 cases.
  • XBRL data covers the main statements only, not footnotes; some tax items are missing for many companies.
  • Companies with a past SEC case are part of the training data.
  • Banks, insurers and funds report differently; ranks for them are less meaningful.
Tax Signal Screen is a research prototype by Pelumi K. Bello, based on her working paper "Do Tax Signals Improve Machine-Learning Detection of Accounting Fraud?" (P. K. Bello). Source data: U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission. Not investment, audit or legal advice.
